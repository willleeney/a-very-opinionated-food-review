Restaurant Popup

Five variations for the restaurant detail view. Each explores a different relationship between photos and reviews. All use the receipt-style edit form (matching Add Place). Key challenge: photos should feel integrated, work with 1 or 100 images, and not hide the reviews.

A — Photo Rail B — Photo-Led Feed C — Split Panel D — Magazine Cards E — Masonry Gallery

Mockup A

Photo Rail + Stacked Reviews

Horizontal scrolling photo rail at the top — first image is taller, the rest scroll sideways. Each photo has its rating + dish name overlaid. "See all" count at the end. Reviews below are simple avatar + text rows. Works identically with 1 photo or 50.

Photo rail scrolls horizontally — naturally handles any number of photos. First image is wider for emphasis. Each photo shows its dish name and rating overlaid at the bottom. A "+N more" slot at the end acts as a gateway to the lightbox.

Reviews below are clean avatar + text rows, completely independent of photos. No layout coupling means the view works identically with 0 or 100 photos.

Edit form is receipt-style (matches Add Place), collapsed by default behind a toggle header.

Scales to any count

Horizontal scroll naturally handles 1-100+ photos. No grid layout to break. The "+N" count always shows how many more exist.

Photos don't push content

Rail has a fixed height (180px). Reviews are always immediately visible below without scrolling past a hero image.

Lightbox on click

Click any rail photo to open a full-screen lightbox with the review details overlaid (rating, reviewer, dish, comment).

Mockup B

Photo-Led Feed

Social/Instagram-style feed. Reviews with photos get a large photo card with the reviewer header above and comment below. Reviews without photos are compact text rows interspersed in the feed. You just scroll — works with any number.

Photo + review = one card. Each review that has a photo gets a full-width image (320px tall) with the reviewer's avatar/name above and their comment/tags below. Rating is overlaid on the photo AND shown next to the name.

No-photo reviews are compact inline rows — they don't take up much space and intersperse naturally.

Infinite scroll feel. This is the only layout where every photo is always visible as you scroll — no lightbox needed just to browse. Click to zoom.

Photos = first class

Photos are large and prominent — this is the most photo-forward layout. Great when food photography is a key part of the review experience.

Scrolling is the interaction

No separate gallery or lightbox to manage. Just scroll. Works perfectly with 1 photo or 50 — the popup just gets taller.

Trade-off: vertical space

Photos take significant height. With many photo-reviews you scroll a lot before seeing text-only reviews. Could add a "show photos" toggle.

Mockup C

Split Panel

Left column: scrollable photo grid (grows vertically with any count). Right column: restaurant info + reviews. Click a photo to highlight the corresponding review. Both panels scroll independently.

Mockup D

Magazine Cards

Each review with a photo is a full-width "magazine spread" — photo with rating + reviewer overlaid at bottom. Comment and tags appear below the photo. Reviews without photos are compact rows. Sort by rating so the best photos (and reviews) are at the top.

Most editorial feel. Like flipping through a food magazine. Each photo-review is a full "spread" with the rating and reviewer overlaid on the image. Matches the publication aesthetic from CLAUDE.md.

Photos are contextual. You see each photo next to its review — no mental mapping between a gallery and a review list. The photo IS the review.

Non-photo reviews are compact rows at the bottom, keeping the visual hierarchy clear: photo reviews > text reviews.

Editorial first

This is the most "publication" feeling layout. Photos are large and dramatic, overlaid text is cinematic. Feels like a food magazine article.

Sorted by impact

Sort by rating descending so the best (most impressive) reviews and photos appear first. Creates a natural visual hierarchy.

Similar to B, more editorial

Compared to B (social feed), this uses the overlay approach instead of header/body separation. Less social, more magazine. No avatars on photo cards.

Mockup E

Masonry Gallery + Review Drawer

Pinterest-style masonry photo grid at the top. Hover shows rating overlay. Click a photo to open a "drawer" below the grid showing that review's full details at large size. Reviews without photos are listed in a compact section below.

Drawer pattern keeps you in context — no separate lightbox or page change. The drawer slides in below the grid, pushes the text-only reviews down. Click another photo to swap.

Text-only section at the bottom collects reviews without photos under a labelled separator. Keeps the gallery clean while ensuring all reviews are accessible.

Gallery browsing

Best layout for quickly scanning all food photos. The masonry grid is compact and visually interesting. Handles 1-100+ photos naturally.

Drawer = inline detail

No overlay or modal-in-modal. The drawer expands inline, keeping you in the popup. Large photo on left, review text on right.
